### The Rot begins
*Important Actors: Mir Jafar, Robert Clive, Miran Jafar, Warren Hastings, Imad-ul-Mulk, Shah Alam, Mir Qasim, Walter Reinhardt *

![[mir_jafar_miran.png]]

The new Nawab (Mir Jafar) was not a particularly good administrator. Given the amount of bribes that the Jagath Seths and the overall leadership had offered to the EIC for the coup and takeover was exorbitant, the rule of the Nawab was doomed from the start. The debts accrued by the Nawab forced the kingdom to be a vassal of the company. This allowed the company to dictate terms especially in the rule of trade and monopolies being set up here. He also assumed the "Kampani" to be an individual rather than an organization. Understandable, given that the concept of a company was quite new. 

Due to his ineffective leadership discontent and rebellions started to rise up. His support base of bankers starting moving away and his army, given that they weren't paid began to pilfer and plunder their own lands. The British used the Nawab's ineffective rule to their advantage by sending their traders to Bengal and imposing their will there. 

### The Glorifying of Warren Hastings
![[Warren_Hastings.png]]

Warren Hastings loved India compared to his predecessor, Clive. Sent to India to work for the EIC only when he was 16, he learnt the local languages to better understand the world he was living in. The issue was that he was the wrong person for the job at the wrong time. Given the weak power of the Nawab, there wasn't much Hastings could do anyways to prevent the enrichment of the British traders taking advantage of the weak Bengali administration. The EIC itself was never tasked with upholding the laws of the land and was merely a company (in name), which enabled this kind of oppression. On one hand the company was merely a trading house but this was all in name. Having its own army and controlling a booming city, these should have been enough signs to realize that a literal state was masquerading as a company. 

Examples of company meddling in various goods trades show how the agents of the company, given their new found powers began to take advantage by using bogus charges to dismantle rival businesses. #observation Warren Hastings did what he could, but he had neither the power nor the influence to make any action implementable on the field. Ultimately, he was mostly just an observer of the decline of the Bengali state. 

### Mughals and Bengal

Shah Alam, given that he was the Heir (soon to be Emperor) was "canvassing" around Awadh trying to garner support in order to take back Mughal control of Bengal. Mughal de jure authority was still respected in these areas and Mughal nobility and unemployed warriors flocked to his standard. Shah Alam's plan was simple, train his army in European ways, gather enough support in and around Bengal and quickly capture these areas to regain Mughal control over it. 

An #observation that I have constantly seen is the post battle disorganization of the Indian armies. Discipline was not strongest forte anyways but Generals and Kings had no control over their armies which essentially led to plundering and ransacking of the cities they planned to capture and rule ([[Chapter 3 - Seeping With the Broom of Plunder#^ce25b3|Ineffective Armies]]) ^e600d1

The Emperor captured Patna initially but EIC and Miran were able to beat them in the second battle due to the disciplined square formation of the Sepoys. The Emperor was almost able to surprise the EIC using a crack cavalry formation to head to undefended Murshidabad but a delay of 3 days no longer made it a surprise allowing the English to catch up.

The Mughals lost the third battle as they couldn't mount an assault of Murshidabad and after their retreat to Patna, an ineffective administration and disorderly army made it hard to get local support for the Mughals. 
### Mughals and Delhi

![[Shahjanabad_old_delhi.png]]

Delhi itself was in perpetual Anarchy. After multiple ransacking and raids by Afghans and Marathas Delhi was a shadow of its former self. No control existed outside its walls and within the walls control was exerted by the Vizier, Imad-ul-Mulk who being paranoid was involved in multiple intrigues, mainly the murder of the Alamgir II and desperately trying to maintain his grip on Delhi. 

Another #critique of the book is the Mughal focus on Delhi. What surprises me is the lack of inclusion of other major Mughal centers of power like Lahore, Peshawar, Multan, etc. Not much is mentioned regarding the loss of Afghan and Punjabi lands from the Mughals and how that contributed to the decline of Mughal power. A lot of focus is given on how Nader Shah kickstarted the decline alongside multiple Maratha and Afghan raids but these seem more like singular events rather than processes that bought the decline. 

### Mir Jafar's overthrow and Mir Qasim's rise

After Miran's random yet suspicious death Mir Jafar had little reason to continue his rule. Given that most of the power was anyways delegated to Miran the administration further plunged into decline. 

![[Mir_Qasim.png]]


After Mir Qasim's interview EIC (Vansittart) found him a suitable replacement for Mir Jafar. Educated, smart and a capable administrator, his proposition of added land to repay the debts Bengal owed gave EIC the impetus to conduct a quick and bloodless coup.After a army mutiny, Mir Jafar was quickly put on a boat and retired to Calcutta with a modest pension. 

Mir Qasim was a capable administrator and turned things around. His examination of state accounts and statements enabled cutting down of corruption and and used that to increase funds for his treasury. He also moved his administration to the fort of Monghyr after realizing Murshidabad and Patna had too much interference from the EIC. His reorganization of the army to a more capable force and his recruitment of Europeans soldiers of fortune (Walter Reinhardt aka _Sumru_) to train his army further strengthened the army.

#observation The book shows how much India was seen as a place of adventure, magic and discovery from the European perspective. The British, French, Portuguese and Dutch were all entering the country with explorers, army recruits and peasants all escaping their countries to make a name for themselves and trying to obtain the riches of the country. An example of this can be Walter Reinhardt aka Sumru, a Alsatian German mercenary who took on the habits of his adopted country. Alongside the various trading companies pulling in second sons of Nobles and various peasants, soldiers of fortune tested their luck in these new waters. Given all the recent military innovations after all the wars fought in continental Europe, the Europeans had entered a relative peace which could have forced these men to discover new lands where they can sell their trade. It reflects the increased Globalization during these times, especially the migration of various Europeans towards Asia/America kickstarting the age of colonization. 

### Legitimizing EIC rule over Bengal

The Mughal emperor; after exchanging a series of letters with the EIC realized they both could have a partnership that will be mutually beneficial. EIC never had any legitimate authority over Indian lands and grants by the Emperor would help assuage any future questions regarding this. The emperor was hoping to be rewarded with coin and support to win back Delhi. 

The Mughal emperor didn't get the support he wanted, or at least was delayed constantly which forced him look for other avenues of support as EIC didn't want to battle the Afghans. It does look like the local warlords/kings didn't mind giving tribute or submission to the Emperor given how decentralized power was in the Indian subcontinent. 

The dual government system could hardly work in peace. The EIC agents abused their positions of power by declining to pay taxes while the Bengali government complained yet received no respite from the EIC. The EIC council's forceful decision of demanding exemption of its authorities from the Bengali government was the final straw. The Bengali administration started to enforce custom duties and taxes on the EIC which caused scuffles. Ellis began to believe he could take on the Bengali army through his European army. That began the last war after which Bengal went under complete control under the EIC.
