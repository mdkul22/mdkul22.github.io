### War with Mir Qasim
_Important Actors: Mir Qasim, Mirza Najaf Khan, Shuja-ud-Daula_

![[mughal_musketeer.png]]

Mir Qasim was forced into a war due to EIC's undue demands of free customs and immunity from Bengal's administration. The EIC council, due to it's members being in debt were forced into war in the chance that they could subjugate Bengal enabling them to rule over this large and prosperous land. This was a financial disaster, ultimately EIC was a trading house and not a state whose objective to expand its territory. A lot of debt had to be taken to fund the army. Added with the increasing casualties dealt thanks to the better trained Bengali army. After the loss of Monghyr, Mir Qasim went in a state of grief and paranoia, unable to trust his generals and filled with suspicion began assassinating his own officers. He began a purge of his most powerful officers (Gurgin Khan, Jagath Seth). After the massacre of the British prisoners and nothing to lose, Qasim withdrew to Awadh were he hoped he could strike an alliance with Shuja-ud-Daula and the Emperor to get back his governorship of Bengal and finally strike a blow to EIC which would prevent them from fomenting any future coups. 

### Shuja-ud-Daula and Mir Qasim

![[Shuja-ud-Daula.png]]

Shuja-ud-Daula, the imposing and ambitious Nawab of Awadh was a proud man. Inspiring loyalty among his followers, he carried charisma but was prone to making rash and impulsive decisions. The Bengali proposal for a triple Mughal alliance excited the Nawab yet made the Emperor anxious as the EIC themselves had pledged themselves as an ally of the Mughals. 

![[naga_sadhus_gossain.png]]

The army was massive: 150k strong but this story is a constantly [[Chapter 4 - A Prince of Little Capacity#^e600d1|repeating one]]. Due to the technological innovations and discipline of the British, numbers didn't matter to much. Even the Gossain's crack troops weren't enough as the weapons they carried alongside their haphazard organization may have inspired fear in older times but now only held the element of slight surprise.
