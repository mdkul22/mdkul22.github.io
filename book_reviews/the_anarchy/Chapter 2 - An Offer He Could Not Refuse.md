Clive was an opportunist, ambitious man whose sole aim was to climb the social ladder whatever the means. Being an excellent soldier and quite the risk taker, his quick and blitz oriented attacks worked well against the technologically backward Mughal armies. He has multiple tenures in India. The first was during the Carnatic wars against the French where he beat  Dupleix’s army and prevented French domination in the south. His stint came in a time when the Marathas ran rampant in Bengal: raiding and pillaging villages and towns around Bengal which enabled the rise of Calcutta as a metropolis thanks to the hesitation of the Marathas to attack the city due to the fear is European defenses and weaponry.

What made the Europeans different was how they, unlike other merchant communities, relied on the ruler’s defenses and had their own security. The main innovation here is monopolizing trade, which enabled merchant communities to become companies, entrenching more power and consolidating resources for their security. Added to this is the culture of not blending with natives, which also made them believe the communities they created were their lands. 

Reading about the history of North India, especially the plains (Delhi), seems to suggest that the cities we talk about are essentially rebuilt every 100 or so years thanks to the invasions they have constantly faced. From the Muslim invasion of the 1100s to the Timurid raids and finally the Mughal invasions of the 1500s, these cities were constantly pillaged, raided, and destroyed. Raids by the Afghans (Durrani) and Persians (Nadir Shah) [[Chapter 1 - 1599#^dce970]]

Mughals were never able to solve the issue of succession. Given the fact that there was no ritualized succession nor strong rules regarding partition, it was essentially left to the ruler in his personal capacity to ensure succession happened successfully. Fratricide wasn’t a cardinal sin, which increased instability further. Given the highly decentralized way power was distributed in India, Mughal hegemony was essentially riding on its technological advantage (cannons, cavalry) during its apex and its wealth purely due to the tributes it bought thanks to bringing the various kings under its dominion. Another issue was how Islam itself didn’t split its clergy or its warrior across different lines, unlike the Hindu or Christian hierarchies. 

Civil wars post-Aurangzeb reduced Mughal power and made it harder for a central authority to consolidate power. Fundamentally speaking, Mughals were outsiders that, though attempting to create a new syncretic culture combining Hindi/Turkic/Muslim culture, weren’t able to completely integrate or create this culture completely. This made it very easy to topple given an attacker who had the ritualized right (Marathas/Vijayanagara) or the technological advancement (British/Persian/Afghan) to do so.
